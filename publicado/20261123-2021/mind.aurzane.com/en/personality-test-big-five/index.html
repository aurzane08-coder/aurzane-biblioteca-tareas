Frequently asked questions

What is the Big Five model?

It is the most widely used personality model in psychological research. It describes personality with five broad, continuous traits: each person has their own level on each one, instead of belonging to a "type".

Which version of the test should I take?

The quick version (20 questions) gives you a fast idea. The classic (50) is the recommended one. The full version (120) adds 30 facets for a much more detailed portrait.

Is this test reliable?

The questions come from the International Personality Item Pool (IPIP), which is public domain and has been used in thousands of studies. Even so, this test is for guidance only: it compares your answers with the range of the scale, not with a population sample, and it cannot be used to diagnose.

What are profiles like "Campfire" or "Lighthouse"?

They are a simple way of summarising your results based on your two highest traits. They are not fixed scientific types: your full result is your five scores.

How is it different from the MBTI or the 16 personalities test?

Those tests assign you a fixed type (for example, "INTJ"). The Big Five measure traits on a continuous scale, the approach with the strongest scientific support.

Do you store my answers?

No. The test runs in your browser and your answers are not sent to any server. If you sign in, you can choose to save your final result to My AURZANE.

        Understand your Big Five profile

        Understanding how your personality works is often the first step towards greater emotional balance and better everyday performance. Unlike rigid labels that sort people into fixed categories, the Big Five model looks at behaviour through continuous dimensions, so it can capture what is unique about each person without oversimplifying.

        This guide helps you read the free AURZANE MIND personality test and turn your scores into practical keys for studying, work and relationships. From a clinical psychology perspective, it is a screening tool for self-knowledge: it points to your strengths and to areas you can work on, without judging you or making a diagnosis.

        What exactly does this test measure?

        The questionnaire assesses five broad dimensions of personality backed by decades of research in differential psychology: openness to experience, conscientiousness, extraversion, agreeableness and neuroticism, or emotional stability.

        Each trait is a spectrum with no better or worse profiles, only different ways of adapting to your environment and relating to everyday life and to other people.

        • Openness: intellectual curiosity, interest in art and willingness to try new ideas.
        • Conscientiousness: organisation, self-discipline and focus on long-term goals.
        • Extraversion: where you get your energy from, sociability and the tendency to seek outside stimulation.
        • Agreeableness: cooperation, empathy and trust in the basic goodness of people.
        • Emotional stability: resilience to stress and how often you feel emotions like anxiety or irritability.

        How to read your results without labelling yourself

        When you get your report, remember that high or low scores on a trait only show general tendencies, not fixed limits on who you are.

        A high score in conscientiousness helps you plan study projects successfully, but it can also turn into perfectionism if you do not manage expectations and rest.

        In the same way, a low score in extraversion does not mean a problem with shyness, but a genuine preference for reflection and for small, quiet social settings.

        • Do not treat your scores as fate.
        • Notice which extremes make you feel good and which ones wear you out every day.
        • Use this snapshot to understand your automatic reactions under pressure.

        Putting it into practice at school and at work

        Knowing your Big Five strengths and challenges lets you design study and work strategies that fit the way you naturally process information and manage your time.

        People high in openness often stand out in creative and research careers, while those high in conscientiousness shine in structured settings that demand rigour.

        If your profile shows low emotional stability, stress management techniques, planned breaks and self-care routines are especially useful to prevent mental exhaustion.

        • Set up your workspace with fewer distractions if your focus is easily disrupted.
        • Tell your classmates, colleagues and managers whether you need more autonomy or more support.
        • Match your career goals to what really motivates you, not to other people's expectations.

        Limits of the test and when to seek professional support

        This free test is for education and self-knowledge only, so it has no clinical validity to diagnose personality disorders or mental health problems.

        If you notice that your personality traits, ongoing anxiety or mood changes seriously interfere with your daily life, your relationships or your work, it is a good idea to see a specialist.

        Clinical psychology offers in-depth assessment and evidence-based therapy to support you through intense distress or a personal crisis.

        • Remember that an online test never replaces the judgement of a licensed psychologist.
        • If you are going through prolonged psychological distress, book an appointment with a mental health professional.
        • If you have thoughts of harming yourself, contact a crisis line now: 988 in the US, Samaritans on 116 123 in the UK and Ireland, or 024 in Spain.

        Frequently asked questions

        What is the difference between the Big Five and tests like the MBTI?

        The Big Five model measures continuous traits on graded scales, which has much stronger support in current psychological research. Tests like the MBTI, by contrast, sort people into fixed types, a less precise approach according to personality science.

        Are my answers stored on an external server?

        No. The whole questionnaire is processed in your web browser, which keeps your data private. Your answers are not stored or sent to any external database, unless you choose to save your final result to your AURZANE account.

        Can my results change over time?

        Yes. Although personality traits are very stable through adulthood, major life experiences, personal growth and therapy can subtly change your scores over time.

        What do names like Lighthouse or Campfire in my result mean?

        They are friendly names that summarise the combination of your two highest Big Five scores. They are not clinical categories, just an accessible way to picture your main tendencies.

        Do I need to sign up to take the personality test?

        No sign-up is needed to complete any version of the test and see your detailed report. You only need to sign in if you want to keep your result card in your AURZANE profile to check it later.
