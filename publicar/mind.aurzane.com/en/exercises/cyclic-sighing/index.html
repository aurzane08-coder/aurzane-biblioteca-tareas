Guided cyclic sighing

Two breaths in through the nose, one after the other, to fill the lungs completely, then a long breath out through the mouth. In a Stanford University study, 5 minutes a day of this breathing improved mood more than meditation.

  • 🌀 Stress
  • 🌤️ Mood
  • ⏱️ 1 to 5 minutes
  • 🔊 Voice-guided

0:00 of 0:00

Cycle: Breathe in through your nose 3 s · Another short breath in 1.5 s · Let it out slowly through your mouth 6 s. The voice comes from your browser; if you cannot hear it, turn up the volume or follow the text and the circle.

How to do it

  1. Breathe in through your nose normally.
  2. Without letting the air out, take a second short breath in to fill your lungs completely.
  3. Let all the air out through your mouth, slowly, until you are empty.
  4. Repeat for 5 minutes.

When to use it: To ease tension in the moment, or as a 5-minute daily practice.

Frequently asked questions

What is the physiological sigh?

It is a breathing pattern the body does on its own when we sigh or cry: two breaths in and a long breath out. It helps release carbon dioxide.

What did the Stanford study show?

Balban and colleagues compared 5 minutes a day of several breathing practices and of meditation for a month. Cyclic sighing improved mood the most and lowered resting breathing rate the most.

How often should I do it?

In the study, 5 minutes a day. For a tense moment, 2 or 3 sighs may be enough.

  • Balban MY, Neri E, Kogon MM, et al. Brief structured respiration practices enhance mood and reduce physiological arousal. Cell Rep Med. 2023;4(1):100895.
  • If at any point you feel dizzy or worse, stop and breathe normally.
  • A wellbeing exercise; it does not replace care from a health professional. If anxiety or insomnia is holding you back, talk to your doctor or a psychologist.
